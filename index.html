Approach

How I decide before I ship

Same process whether it's 7am or midnight and I'm tired. Think about what breaks first, find the bottleneck, prove the graph moved, and only ship what survives contact with reality.

01

Correctness first

Before I think about speed or clever design, I ask: what happens on a retry, a duplicate delivery, a partial failure? Usage and billing-adjacent paths can't quietly drift, so I default to explicit state and errors I can recover from.

02

Measure the bottleneck

Focus beats motivation when the bottleneck is real — p99 in a transform path, deploy friction slowing three teams down, a blind spot hiding retries. Name it, fix it, show the graph moved.

03

Invert the failure

Ask what breaks at 10x traffic, a bad deploy, or a confused teammate downstream — then design that version of the story out of existence before it happens. Most of my best decisions started as a failure I just refused to let happen.

04

Design for who inherits it

Platform work pays off when the next team’s safe path is also the easy path — contracts they don't have to guess at, releases that don't need a rollback plan, and docs that say what's broken instead of hiding it. In an incident I stay on the fix, not the blame, and step back once someone else can own it.
